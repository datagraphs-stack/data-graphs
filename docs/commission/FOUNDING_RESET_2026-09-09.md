# DataGraphs.ai — Canonical Founding Reset & Business Blueprint

**Founding reset date:** September 9, 2026

## 1. Executive decision

The existing DataGraphs.ai product direction is superseded.

DataGraphs.ai is **not primarily a CSV upload-and-analysis SaaS**.

The existing CSV Studio may remain operational as a demonstration, laboratory and future bring-your-own-data interface, but it must no longer define the company, homepage, architecture roadmap, market positioning or commercial thesis.

DataGraphs.ai is being built as an:

# INTERNET DATA FOUNDRY AND GRAPH INTELLIGENCE PLATFORM

Its purpose is to continuously acquire useful public data from the internet, preserve the underlying evidence, transform raw web information into structured entities/facts/relationships/events, resolve those records into durable graph identities, track how those graphs change through time, and use those graphs to power commercial intelligence products, APIs, datasets and future businesses.

The fundamental company loop is:

**DISCOVER → ACQUIRE → ARCHIVE → EXTRACT → RESOLVE → GRAPH → MONITOR → SIGNAL → PRODUCT**

The goal is not to create graphs that merely look interesting.

The goal is to manufacture **reusable structured intelligence assets**.

---

# 2. Mission

DataGraphs turns the fragmented public internet into structured, temporal, provenance-backed graphs that humans, applications and agents can use as intelligence.

The web contains enormous quantities of useful information, but most of it exists as:

- pages;
- PDFs;
- listings;
- tables;
- articles;
- directories;
- feeds;
- registries;
- announcements;
- product pages;
- documentation;
- pricing pages;
- job postings;
- government publications;
- company websites.

That information is useful individually.

It becomes dramatically more valuable when it is:

- continuously collected;
- historically preserved;
- normalized;
- connected to durable entities;
- reconciled across sources;
- linked through relationships;
- tracked through time;
- converted into meaningful events and signals.

That transformation is DataGraphs.

# 3. What a DataGraph actually is

A DataGraph is not a chart.

A DataGraph is not a CSV.

A DataGraph is not a database table.

A DataGraph is not an AI conversation.

A DataGraph is a **versioned intelligence object** representing some portion of the real world.

A mature DataGraph can contain:

- entities;
- entity aliases;
- attributes;
- relationships;
- events;
- observations;
- claims;
- supporting sources;
- immutable source snapshots;
- temporal validity;
- provenance;
- confidence;
- contradictions;
- derived metrics;
- change history;
- computed signals;
- projections/views intended for particular products.

Examples:

**Company DataGraph**
Company → products → executives → locations → technologies → jobs → pricing → funding → partners → announcements → competitors.

**Product DataGraph**
Product → seller → price → availability → specifications → reviews → category → competitors → price history.

**Regulatory DataGraph**
Jurisdiction → agency → requirement → rule → activity → business → source evidence.

**Property DataGraph**
Property → observations → sales → corrections → ownership-related public facts → location → events → market signals.

**Industry DataGraph**
Companies → people → products → capital → partnerships → hiring → technologies → events → market structure.

The persistent asset is the graph and its history.

The interface is replaceable.

# 4. The core trust architecture

The existing project discovered an important principle that should survive the reset: data outputs should have reconstruction paths.

The new canonical chain is:

**SOURCE**
**→ SNAPSHOT**
**→ OBSERVATION**
**→ CLAIM / FACT**
**→ ENTITY**
**→ RELATIONSHIP**
**→ GRAPH STATE**
**→ EVENT / SIGNAL**
**→ INTELLIGENCE PRODUCT**

## SOURCE

Where the information originated.

## SNAPSHOT

Exact evidence acquired at a particular time.

Raw source evidence should be preserved when legally and operationally appropriate.

## OBSERVATION

Something extracted from one specific source snapshot.

An observation does not automatically become universal truth.

## CLAIM / FACT

A structured assertion made by a source or derived through an explicitly defined deterministic transformation.

Conflicting claims may coexist.

## ENTITY

A durable real-world identity such as a company, product, location, organization, regulation or property.

## RELATIONSHIP

A meaningful link between entities.

Examples:

- company → sells → product;
- company → operates_in → country;
- company → hired → executive;
- company → launched → product;
- requirement → governed_by → agency.

## GRAPH STATE

The best currently supported representation of entities and relationships under defined resolution policies.

Historical states remain reconstructable.

## EVENT / SIGNAL

A meaningful change inferred from the temporal graph.

Examples:

- competitor increased price;
- company entered a market;
- company opened 34 engineering roles;
- executive changed;
- regulation changed;
- product disappeared;
- new product category emerged;
- company partnership appeared.

## INTELLIGENCE PRODUCT

A customer-facing service that converts those graph assets into decisions, discovery, monitoring or automation.

# 5. AI doctrine

AI is an accelerator inside the foundry.

It is not the evidence.

Appropriate AI tasks include:

- page/document classification;
- extraction candidate generation;
- ontology mapping;
- relationship candidate generation;
- entity-match candidates;
- semantic normalization;
- change summarization;
- event classification;
- query interpretation;
- narrative generation.

AI output must preserve provenance.

Material graph assertions should identify where they originated.

Entity resolution must preserve uncertainty rather than silently fusing ambiguous identities.

Deterministic logic should be used whenever a deterministic operation is possible.

The existing DataGraphs deterministic computation engine remains useful for derived metrics and calculations over graph data.

# 6. DataGraphs is a foundry before it is an application

The company should be understood as several stacked systems.

## Layer A — Acquisition Fabric

Discovers and retrieves public internet sources.

Capabilities eventually include:

- seed URL discovery;
- domain crawling;
- sitemap ingestion;
- structured feeds;
- APIs;
- government/open datasets;
- Common Crawl and other permitted bulk corpora;
- browser-rendered retrieval where justified;
- scheduled refresh;
- source priority;
- crawl budgets;
- rate limits;
- retry/error management.

## Layer B — Evidence Archive

Preserves source identity and history.

Capabilities:

- immutable raw snapshots;
- timestamps;
- hashes;
- HTTP metadata;
- source metadata;
- meaningful-content fingerprints;
- version history;
- duplicate detection;
- change detection.

## Layer C — Extraction Fabric

Transforms sources into structured observations.

Capabilities:

- deterministic parsers where possible;
- schema extraction;
- structured-data extraction;
- AI-assisted extraction;
- document classification;
- normalized claims;
- source spans/references;
- confidence.

## Layer D — Identity & Resolution Engine

Answers:

> Are these two observations referring to the same real-world thing?

Capabilities:

- canonical IDs;
- aliases;
- deterministic exact matches;
- source-specific identifiers;
- scored candidate matches;
- conflict detection;
- merge/split history;
- human review where necessary;
- reversible decisions.

## Layer E — Graph Compiler

Turns resolved observations into usable domain graphs.

Capabilities:

- entities;
- edges;
- facts;
- temporal properties;
- provenance;
- event histories;
- graph projections;
- domain ontologies.

## Layer F — Change & Signal Engine

Continuously compares graph history.

It should detect meaningful change rather than merely changed HTML.

Examples:

- new company;
- disappeared product;
- changed price;
- changed executive;
- new job cluster;
- funding announcement;
- new office;
- regulatory amendment;
- altered product capability;
- new competitor relationship.

## Layer G — Intelligence Delivery

Graph assets become usable through:

- public intelligence pages;
- search;
- explorers;
- dashboards;
- alerts;
- reports;
- APIs;
- graph feeds;
- bulk datasets;
- SDKs;
- MCP/agent interfaces;
- embedded intelligence.

## Layer H — Intelligence Businesses

Purpose-built commercial products can be launched on top of one or more DataGraphs.

This is where the largest value can ultimately be captured.

# 7. The business model

DataGraphs should not depend on one SaaS subscription.

It should support several progressively valuable commercial layers.

## Business Model 1 — Vertical intelligence products

This should be the preferred route to early revenue.

Build an expensive proprietary graph once.

Sell its conclusions and monitoring repeatedly.

Possible products:

- market intelligence;
- competitor intelligence;
- company intelligence;
- pricing intelligence;
- supplier intelligence;
- regulatory intelligence;
- property intelligence;
- product intelligence;
- regional economic intelligence.

Customers pay for the intelligence, not the crawler.

Potential packaging:

**Free**
Public graph exploration and limited intelligence.

**Professional**
History, monitoring, saved views, alerts, exports.

**Team**
Shared workspaces, larger graph access, deeper signals.

**Business / Enterprise**
API, bulk export, custom feeds, broader history, integration and support.

Exact prices remain hypotheses until a specific intelligence product is validated.

## Business Model 2 — Graph APIs

Customers consume structured entities, relationships, events and changes directly.

Pricing can be based on:

- API usage;
- records;
- graph entities;
- refresh frequency;
- event volume;
- dataset size;
- commercial licence.

## Business Model 3 — Data feeds and datasets

Offer maintained structured datasets or graph snapshots.

The premium is freshness + structure + provenance + historical continuity.

## Business Model 4 — Managed DataGraph builds

A customer can commission:

> Build and continuously maintain a graph of this market/domain/ecosystem.

This can support meaningful B2B contracts long before DataGraphs has internet-wide coverage.

## Business Model 5 — Internal portfolio infrastructure

DataGraphs should eventually become reusable infrastructure for other intelligence companies in the portfolio.

BusinessPermitGuide is effectively a regulatory graph business.

Averdane is effectively a property graph business.

W3Africa is effectively an African knowledge/market graph business.

They should not be forcibly rewritten around DataGraphs today.

But the horizontal primitives developed in those projects—source acquisition, immutable evidence, provenance, identity resolution, change monitoring and deterministic computation—should increasingly converge into reusable DataGraphs capabilities.

Future businesses should become much cheaper to launch because the foundry already exists.

# 9. The first proving graph

Do not attempt to crawl the whole internet.

The doctrine remains:

> Think enormous. Prove narrowly.

The first new proving target should be:

# ONE PERFECT 100-COMPANY MARKET GRAPH

Choose one technology/business market with information available primarily from public first-party company sources.

The initial graph should contain approximately 100 organizations and cover, where supported:

- canonical company identity;
- official website;
- aliases;
- headquarters/location;
- products;
- product categories;
- pricing pages;
- documentation;
- company news/blog;
- major announcements;
- careers/jobs;
- leadership where publicly stated;
- partnerships where directly supported;
- relevant public events;
- source evidence for material claims;
- historical change.

The proving graph should crawl multiple source pages per company and continuously monitor them.

The exact initial market is less important than proving the machinery.

A technology/data/AI infrastructure market is a reasonable proving environment because product, pricing, docs, releases and hiring signals are commonly expressed on public company sites.

The first graph must NOT depend on scraping social networks or protected/private sources.

# 13. Technical strategy

Continue using GitHub and Cloudflare as the primary engineering/deployment platforms.

The initial architecture should favor:

### Cloudflare Workers

Acquisition endpoints, APIs, extraction orchestration and graph services.

### Cloudflare Workflows

Long-lived crawl and processing jobs.

### Cloudflare Queues

Fan-out/fan-in page acquisition and extraction workloads where appropriate.

### Cloudflare R2

Raw source archive, larger artifacts and eventually bulk graph/data exports.

### Cloudflare D1

Source registry, metadata, graph entities/relationships, claims, task state and smaller materialized projections during the proving stage.

### Workers AI

Candidate extraction/classification/resolution where useful.

### Browser Rendering

Only when authoritative public information genuinely requires rendered-browser access.

Do not prematurely engineer for billions of entities.

Prove the pipeline at hundreds, then thousands, then millions.

Architecture should preserve the ability to split specialized storage/query components later when real measured scale requires it.

---

# 14. Source and compliance philosophy

DataGraphs should begin with information that is clearly public and commercially sensible to process.

Prefer:

- official company websites;
- government/open data;
- public filings;
- public documentation;
- public press releases;
- public product/pricing pages;
- openly accessible feeds;
- permitted bulk/open datasets.

Do not make anti-bot circumvention the core architecture.

Do not begin with sensitive personal profiles.

Do not republish entire copyrighted pages as the product.

Store source evidence for internal provenance where appropriate and expose structured facts, citations, links, summaries and derived intelligence.

Implement source-level crawl policies, budgets, rate controls and removal/opt-out handling as the foundry matures.

# 15. Canonical development roadmap

## Phase 0 — Founding realignment

Replace the erroneous CSV-first company strategy.

Preserve it only as historical context.

Deliver:

- new product vision;
- new founding record;
- new roadmap;
- new architecture map;
- ADR superseding the old founding customer/wedge;
- explicit classification of existing Studio as Lab/demo.

Exit when every future agent can explain DataGraphs correctly in one paragraph.

## Phase 1 — Source Registry

Create canonical source/domain/page identities and crawl policy.

Exit when a bounded list of company domains can be registered, scheduled and inspected.

## Phase 2 — Acquisition Fabric

Build bounded site crawling.

Seed URL → discover eligible pages → fetch → archive.

Exit when multiple real company domains can be crawled repeatably without manual page lists.

## Phase 3 — Evidence Archive

Preserve exact source snapshots and meaningful fingerprints.

Exit when unchanged, cosmetically changed and materially changed pages can be distinguished conservatively.

## Phase 4 — Structured Observation Layer

Extract useful structured claims from representative company pages.

Exit when products, pricing-related observations, announcements and selected company attributes carry source provenance.

## Phase 5 — Entity & Identity Layer

Create durable organizations/products and map observations to them.

Exit when repeated crawls update existing graph identities rather than creating duplicates.

## Phase 6 — Relationship Graph

Create versioned graph relationships.

Exit when company → product, company → location, company → announcement and other proven relationships are queryable.

## Phase 7 — Temporal/Event Graph

Convert meaningful graph changes into events.

Exit when the system can say not merely “HTML changed” but “Company X launched Product Y” or “Pricing page changed,” with evidence.

## Phase 8 — 100-Company Proving Graph

Populate one complete bounded market.

Exit when coverage, provenance, refresh and entity quality meet published internal thresholds.

## Phase 9 — Market Graph Product

Expose the proving graph through a serious public intelligence experience.

Exit when a stranger can understand the market without knowing anything about the underlying crawler.

## Phase 10 — Monitoring & Signals

Scheduled refresh plus saved entities/markets and signal delivery.

Exit when a customer can follow a market and receive meaningful changes.

## Phase 11 — Commercial API / Data Feed

Expose controlled graph/query/export interfaces.

## Phase 12 — Second Graph Domain

Prove that the foundry is reusable by building a materially different graph without rewriting the entire acquisition and provenance stack.

## Phase 13 — Graph Product Factory

Formalize reusable modules so new intelligence businesses can be bootstrapped rapidly from shared infrastructure.

## Phase 14 — Scale

Only now optimize for large crawl volume, broad source coverage and very large graph storage.

# 16. Definition of the new MVP

The MVP is NOT:

> upload a CSV and get a chart.

The new MVP is complete when:

1. DataGraphs autonomously crawls a bounded real market from public sources;
2. source snapshots are archived with provenance;
3. useful observations are extracted;
4. observations resolve into durable entities;
5. relationships create a useful graph;
6. repeated crawls preserve identity and history;
7. meaningful changes become events/signals;
8. at least one public intelligence interface consumes the graph;
9. every material displayed claim can be traced to evidence;
10. the resulting product is valuable even if the user never uploads a file.

That is the first true DataGraphs MVP.
